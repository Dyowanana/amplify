# P022 – Rock paper scissors myth duel

## 1. Source
- Instagram carousel by @brightbraintech, 5 slides, caption "Rock, paper, scissors...but make it marketing!!". Collected 2026-10-14 from the owner's phone screenshots (slides 1 and 2 only), imported with `fetch_insta.py --file` as `content-bank/_sources/ig_brightbraintech/manual-20261014-003659`.

## 2. Summary
A quick game per slide: the viewer reads both hands, works out which gesture wins, and the winner's line lands as the lesson.

## 3. Best for
- Any niche with "what people think works vs. what actually works" pairs: marketing, founders, fitness, money, study habits.
- Accounts that want saves and shares from peers ("this is so me").
- Not for: step-by-step how-tos, product demos, anything that needs proof or numbers per slide.

## 4. Structure
5 to 8 slides.

| # | Role | What happens | Text on screen (pattern) |
|---|------|--------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Hook / cover | Challenge headline in the top third, a "let's play" label with a thin arrow, two hands mid-game (rock vs. scissors in the source), the account mark at the bottom | `think you know [topic]?` · `let's play →` |
| 2 to N | Round | Same two-hand layout. The left hand makes the losing gesture with the common belief above it; the right hand makes the gesture that beats it with the better move under it | `[the belief / the habit people default to]` · `[the move that actually wins]` |

Round logic is real: paper beats rock, scissors beat paper, rock beats scissors. The right hand always wins. Rotate the pairs so no two rounds in a row use the same gestures.

## 9. Hook formula(s)
- **Challenge to the viewer's know-how:** `think you know [topic]?` + `let's play`.
- **Game call + edition:** `rock, paper, scissors, shoot` + `[audience] edition` (the trend's own words, may stay word for word).
- Mechanic: the question pokes pride, the standoff promises a winner per slide, so people swipe to play.
- Evidence: "THINK YOU KNOW MARKETING?" / "Let's play".

## 10. Slide text rules
- Rounds: 3 to 7 words per side, a statement, no question. The losing side names the default habit or belief; the winning side names the better move in the same grammar where possible.
- Lowercase (the source used ALL CAPS; the owner's rule makes it lowercase). No dashes, colons or semicolons. No emoji on slides.

## 11. Visual style
**Visual class:** `other:hand-gesture-duel-card`.
- **Canvas** 4:5, 1080x1350. A flat, fine-textured background in one colour (red felt #c43a42 in the source).
- **Hands:** photographed hands cut out with clean, hard edges, no cast shadow. Left hand enters from the left edge with a sleeve showing at the edge; right hand enters from the right edge. Both at the same height, meeting in the middle with a 6 to 10% gap. Cover: hands at 52 to 84% of the height; rounds: 40 to 73%. Each hand about 40 to 53% of the width.
- **Text:** bold neo-grotesque, tight tracking, white, no box, no outline. Cover headline about 5% cap height centred at 15 to 30%; "let's play" in a light weight at about 44% with a thin arrow to the right. Round labels about 2.2% cap height: the losing line centred above the left hand (about 32 to 39%), the winning line right-aligned under the right hand (about 66 to 70%).
- **Recurring elements:** the account mark (bottom centre in the source), an editing-UI motif on the cover (an edit menu and a pixel cursor on the headline).
- **Fixed:** two hands from opposite edges at one height, one flat texture, white bold type, the diagonal text placement (upper left, lower right).
- **Varies:** background colour, the gestures per round, the hands' styling (sleeve, jewellery).

## 12. Caption formula and hashtag pattern

## 14. Do / Don't
- Do: make every gesture read instantly (a clear fist, a flat open hand, a clean V); check finger counts on every generated hand; keep the winner on the right.
- Don't: let a round's logic break (the "winning" hand must beat the other); put text inside generated images; copy the source's hands, jewellery, red felt or the iOS edit menu as is.
- **Originality:** new hands and styling, new background, new statements. The game, the two-edge layout and the diagonal labels are the format.
- **AI production:** generate the 3 gestures for each side once (6 images, the same person and sleeve, on flat chroma green), key them out in code and reuse them across slides; draw the background, the text and any UI motif in code (`rpscards.py`).
